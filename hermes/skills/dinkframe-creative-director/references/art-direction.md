# DINKFRAME art-direction playbook

## Creative standard

Every prompt must read like instructions from a professional creative director
to a production designer. Make deliberate choices, explain them through direct
visual instructions, and protect the client facts. Avoid generic "make it
epic" language and disconnected effect lists.

## Intent and hierarchy

### Competition or announcement

Lead with the athlete, player name, and tournament identity. Use event modules,
date, location, and partner information as supporting layers. Create movement
and anticipation without making the page resemble a generic event flyer.

### Achievement or congratulations

Lead with the athlete and result. Treat a podium or celebration photograph as
evidence of the moment, often atmospheric behind a sharper hero image. Make the
medal, placement, or win legible at thumbnail size. Avoid future-tense event
advertising and obvious celebration cliches.

## Concept families

Choose one family, then adapt it to the supplied logo and photos.

- **Minimalist:** restrained geometry, strong type, generous negative space,
  one controlled accent.
- **Clean & Premium:** refined editorial grid, elegant spacing, tactile detail,
  polished but quiet lighting.
- **Powerful / Athletic:** asymmetric movement, bold condensed type, controlled
  speed lines, court geometry, strong contrast.
- **Futuristic:** technical grids, synthetic depth, angular systems, restrained
  glow, precise digital texture.
- **Cyberpunk / Neon:** night palette, luminous color, digital atmosphere; keep
  the athlete natural and the information readable.
- **Luxury Sports:** expensive materials, disciplined lighting, deep neutrals,
  metallic accents, minimal ornament.
- **Editorial:** magazine-cover hierarchy, oversized cropped type, fine rules,
  confident photography, asymmetric whitespace.
- **Urban:** raw type scale, paper or concrete texture, street-poster layering,
  deliberate imperfections.
- **Cinematic:** dramatic key light, atmospheric depth, film-poster framing,
  restrained particles and lens effects.
- **Japanese-inspired:** disciplined balance, graphic asymmetry, bold flat
  shapes, purposeful emptiness; avoid stereotypical symbols.
- **Tropical:** warm light, expressive color, organic movement, sophisticated
  regional energy rather than novelty graphics.
- **Experimental / Surprise:** derive a concept from the strongest visual cue
  in the event logo, photography, result, or venue. Name the concept and keep
  it internally consistent.

## Required prompt architecture

Use the following sequence when applicable:

1. Objective, subject, tournament, and final format
2. Named creative concept and emotional tone
3. Palette and material language
4. Background environment and depth system
5. Player-photo roles and identity-preservation rules
6. Main name or achievement typography
7. Event, partner, date, location, and handle hierarchy
8. Tournament-logo placement and preservation
9. Overall composition and visual flow
10. Sponsor-safe negative space
11. Strict avoidances
12. Final hierarchy and output contract

Do not force every section to the same length. Spend detail on the parts that
control the result: image roles, hierarchy, composition, typography, and visual
restraint.

## Photography rules

- Preserve identity, face, hair, skin tone, body proportions, clothing,
  equipment, and natural anatomy.
- Do not merge two people or transfer clothing and equipment between images.
- Keep the hero athlete crisp and dimensional.
- Use secondary photographs intentionally: faded memory, environmental layer,
  action echo, or partner context--not as random collage filler.
- Do not over-recolor skin or turn photography into illustration unless the
  client explicitly requests an illustrative style.

## Typography and information

Use two type personalities at most: a display face and a supporting grotesk or
editorial sans. Make the player name or result readable at Instagram thumbnail
size. Prefer short editorial labels to crowded information boxes. Never invent
copy. Spell names, handles, events, dates, and locations exactly as supplied.

## Brand and sponsor rules

Treat the event logo as protected artwork: do not redraw, recolor, distort, or
replace it. Its shapes and colors may inspire the wider visual system without
repeating the logo excessively. Never generate fictional sponsors. Reserve one
or more calm, high-contrast zones for sponsor logos to be placed manually.

## Quality controls

Reject or revise a direction when it has:

- several unrelated visual styles;
- effects without a hierarchy or narrative purpose;
- a generic stadium or tournament-template background;
- unreadable event details or excessive microcopy;
- more glow, particles, gradients, or panels than the concept needs;
- altered faces, anatomy, clothing, paddles, partners, or logos;
- invented sponsors, results, dates, or tournament information;
- decorative clutter in the sponsor-safe areas;
- a composition that cannot survive a centered 4:5 crop.

Before returning the prompt, silently check factual fidelity, visual hierarchy,
identity protection, logo protection, sponsor policy, 4:5 crop safety, and
whether the concept feels specific to this order.
